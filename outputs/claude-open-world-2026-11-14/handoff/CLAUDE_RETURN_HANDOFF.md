## Colossal temple and tree

The current Blender study uses studs as units. It has five offset main pavilions, side shrines and suspended galleries, a broad 138-step stair, guardian lions, amber lanterns, weathered red lacquer, dark curved tile roofs, gold wheels and trims, terraced masonry, hanging vines, a bare tree with roots spanning the terraces, and a stormy portrait staging camera. The stair rises roughly 86 studs. Its lower width is about 134 studs, tapering to around 83 studs near the threshold. The tree’s highest authored primary control point is at Z 526, before its finer tips. These are staging dimensions and need a world-space fit check before export.

### What changed in the tree

- `organic_path` uses Catmull–Rom curves, transported cross-sections that stay stable on near-vertical runs, lopsided cambium profiles, and tapered roots and limbs. Structural branch starts go inside the trunk or parent limb.
- The bark sweep end caps are welded and checked for manifold edges before Blender’s 0.92-stud voxel union. The result is smoothed four passes, separated islands are removed, and manifold topology is checked again. The main bark finished as one connected surface with 419,122 vertices. Very fine twigs remain separate render geometry, sharing the bark material. This is the high-detail study mesh.
- The render has stronger trunk offsets, a broader root sweep, more crown forks, a second anisotropic bark bump layer, and softer fog edges. The broad junctions now meet continuously in the main render. The surface still needs an art pass.

### What to change next, in order

1. **Sculpt the tree as the hero asset.** The render still reads as an even, smooth fan with overly upright, broom-like forks. Build a clear dominant trunk, two or three thick asymmetric leaders and a few long lateral limbs that cross the portrait at different heights. Vary the taper over each curve and taper secondary limbs much earlier. Put smaller forks at irregular intervals and angles, including outward and downward branches. Use the supplied reference’s tangled silhouettes and negative spaces. Check both front and side to catch gaps, slivers or hairline junctions in the separate fine twigs.
2. **Put grain into the form.** The broad bark is too uniform, the bump reads as mottled stone, and some grooves and shadows on the lower flank read as holes. Sculpt deep continuous longitudinal bark flutes and scars that follow the bend of each limb. Carry bark ridges from trunk into the branch crotches and blend those channels through the union. Use pale ridges, darker recesses, broken plates, moss at ground contact and restrained weathering. Keep junctions uneven and broadly tapered. A single world-axis noise field cannot follow the turns. Review close renders of at least the two primary crown forks and one root junction from more than one direction.
3. **Replace the lower slab impression with load-bearing roots.** Many visible roots currently flatten together into a pale mass just right of the entrance in camera. Reframe or reroute the highest-contrast roots so their sweep dominates the left-hand terraces as in the reference. Let roots arch over, squeeze around and disappear behind individual stone courses. Break them into smaller forks where they grip retaining walls. Show greenery in those contacts. Preserve the broad stair and clear player route.
4. **Match the reference composition and age the architecture.** The figure and stair scale work as a baseline. The left guardian is nearly out of frame, the stairs and lower wall feel too orderly and the doorway reads as evenly spaced posts. Bring both weathered guardian silhouettes into view, add individual chipped blocks, irregular cracked treads, roots and leaf litter at the margins, visible tiled eaves with damaged edges, hand-carved relief and beam ornament, rafts of moss, and layered galleries. Shape the entrance into the ornate warm focal point beneath a dark silhouette. Improve depth with crag silhouettes, intermittent mist and a readable cool key against warm lanterns. Stage a portrait from the foot of the stair and a player-eye-height approach. Use the exact creator image side by side at each checkpoint.
5. **Preserve playability and protect the rest of the world.** Complete the temple path and rim connection before selecting final footprints. The old hall came in at 90.4 studs wide and the old spec asked for about 120 across the complex. The creator now explicitly wants Vesperhold-scale landmarks and larger arenas. Begin with the enlarged survey and validate the actual revised geometry and play space instead of applying the old 120-stud number.

The scale survey found room to test a **1400 × 1120** stud basin near X/Z **−4178 / −2298**, with `Back = 750` in the existing Bellwood orientation. This is roughly 15 times the area of the current 350 × 300 basin. Vesperhold’s principal ridge span is roughly 1,660 studs. The old `sightlines.luau` probe returned 0/40 visible checks for eight road positions against five target points on the earlier 320-stud hall/roughly 250-stud tree composition, with the proposed rim lift. That result does not cover the much taller reference study. Rerun the survey, road reveal, neighbor routes and stream crossing checks with final bounds before committing a carve. Widen and reshape the bore/exit or add a passage if that composition needs it. Live layout was left at the existing size.
