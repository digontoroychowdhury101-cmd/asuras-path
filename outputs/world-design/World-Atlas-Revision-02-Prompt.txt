Use case: stylized-concept.
Asset: revised world atlas for an original Roblox soulslike action RPG. Create ONE beautiful, carefully composed 3840x2160 landscape cartographic illustration. This is a substantial change of art direction: a flat ink-and-wash game atlas with deliberate geography and believable places, not a cinematic 3D miniature panorama.

ART DIRECTION: the restrained, tactile quality of an old engraved topographical survey, subtly painted by a fantasy concept artist. Fine dark-brown ink contours and mountain hachures, small individually drawn ordinary settlements, controlled stippled woods, faded olive fields, grey-green marsh, chalk-white exposed lakebed, cold grey northern rock, and pale blue-grey western sea on warm muted vellum. Light paper grain, irregular penwork, purposeful selective detail. Calm, legible, austere and beautiful. No luminous gold accents, painterly bloom, candy-colored biome patches, giant cathedral dioramas or fantasy postcard lighting. This is newly generated concept artwork in an ink-and-wash aesthetic, not a photograph of an actual historic map. No claim of human authorship in the image.

DESIGN INSPIRATION: draw on Elden Ring's contrast of spacious overworld and dense multi-level dungeon spaces, Sekiro's layered mountain routes and views back to earlier places, and Bloodborne's oppressive, socially specific city districts. Do not copy any of their map shapes, locations, iconography or characters. Design one original historically coherent kingdom. Upland timber architecture and lowland masonry share the same materials, tools, emblems and building traditions. Do not paste a Japanese temple biome next to a Victorian city biome.

GEOGRAPHY: an asymmetrical country with a rough WESTERN coast, gentle southern farmland, a forested northern mountain arc climbing toward the NORTHEAST, a central fortified ridge city, and an exposed former inland lakebed in the EAST. A great dam stands between the eastern basin and the western river outlet. Its river descends past low city districts and a southern river town to the western estuary. The crown drained the lake, flooding downstream workers' districts and exposing a buried city. Remnant rivers descend logically from northern high ground. Fields, roads, villages, quarries, canals and docks reflect this history. The landscape must have substantial open terrain and ordinary settlement between major landmarks. Buildings are small cartographic architectural drawings in plan or slight conventional map perspective; terrain is seen from above. No aerial 3D camera horizon.

TWELVE SURFACE REGIONS, each with its own exact, legible, elegantly spaced dark serif label. These are twelve distinct places, not twelve symmetrical biome wedges:
'LONGFIELDS' across the south/southwest: hedged farms, grazing land, tiny hamlets, windmills, a military earthwork. Give the southern countryside enough space to feel explorable.
'BELLWATER TOWN' just inland of the southern river estuary: an irregular working market town with narrow lanes, workshops, a bridge and surrounding fields. A small walled abbey sanctuary stands on a defensible hill at its edge, marked by one discreet rest symbol. Its tiny side courtyard is the optional duel area, not a dominant world landmark.
'WRECKERS’ COAST' along the western cliffs: a river mouth, tide coves, ropewalk sheds, scattered wrecks and one small beacon fort. Follow a natural irregular shoreline rather than a straight decorative border.
'OLD WARD' in the central-western lower capital: dense crooked brick street blocks, yards, an old hospital, kilns, flood marks and canal banks. The district has irregular roof clusters and real urban grain, not a circle of identical castles.
'HIGH WARD' immediately above Old Ward on the central ridge: terraced streets, copper-roof buildings, restrained formal gardens and a single substantial administrative palace. The capital is a layered city of ordinary buildings; the palace does not consume the whole district.
'WEIRLANDS' at the former lake's WESTERN outlet, east of the capital: one enormous but credible masonry dam, chains, pump houses, channel beds, workers' huts, sluice stairs and a small abandoned chapel.
'REED COUNTRY' in the southeast below the former shore: dark reeds, stilt hamlets, dry boardwalks, fishing platforms, shallow pools and an abandoned bath complex. Use elegant map symbols and dense reed hatching, not fluorescent swamp colors.
'QUARRY HILLS' on the northwest side of the capital: angular quarry cuts, lime roads, worker housing, abandoned carts and a steep convict stair. Its stone matches the capital's construction.
'PINE MARCH' across the north-central foothills: deep evergreen woods broken by charcoal clearings, ravines, an old border road and a modest fort. Broad patches of quiet forest matter as much as ruins.
'THOUSAND STEPS' on the northeast mountain shoulder below the highest pass: switchback paths, cedar-built hillside villages, terraced retaining walls, a bellmakers' village and one hanging temple compound fitted into a cliff. Buildings look like an upland tradition of this same kingdom.
'WHITE PASS' at the northern/northeastern heights: thin snow on high ridges, avalanche scars, a hospice and a compact old defensive keep. Snow follows elevation rather than an arbitrary region boundary.
'SALT BASIN' in the exposed eastern lakebed: enormous pale dry flats, old shorelines, stranded ships, abandoned mooring posts, former islands standing above the bed, archaeological trenches and an old dry harbour. The strongest open-space contrast in the map. Do not fill its flats with buildings.

UNDERGROUND: include one modest, clearly delineated cartographic inset in the lower-left sea margin, taking no more than one fifth of the overall image. Label its two connected underground districts exactly 'DEEPWORKS' and 'FIRST CITY'. Deepworks is a drawn tunnel network beneath the dam and city, with pump galleries and a quarry shaft. First City is an excavated ancient street plan below the salt basin, with a covered avenue, archive and sealed court. Use subtle stair/lift symbols that correspond to surface entrances, and a fine ink tunnel joining these two underground regions. This is a conventional underground map inset, not an exposed front-facing glowing cavern or a disconnected fantasy continent. Main surface geography remains dominant.

ROUTE LOGIC: compose actual fine surveyed road and footpath lines that follow valleys, contours, ridgelines, bridges and city gates. Give each surface region several small distinct local sites, roughly three per region, drawn as ruins, hamlets, works, caves or shrines. Keep small local sites unlabeled to preserve legibility. Longfields links town, coast and reeds. Town links Old Ward. Coast links quarry. Quarry links forest. Forest links upper city and mountain steps. Old and High Wards are connected by terraces and a gated stair. Old Ward links dam workers' paths; High Ward reaches the dam crest. Dam and reeds each lead separately into the salt basin. Basin climbs into Thousand Steps; the steps climb White Pass. Two or three understated secondary routes form useful loops. A few tunnel segments may be dotted, but do not drown the map in spaghetti lines. There is no equal-spoke central hub and no bridges crossing every gap. Preserve some dead ends, hidden side valleys and inaccessible high ground.

TYPE AND PRESENTATION: small restrained engraved title 'BELLWATER' at an upper corner; simple north arrow, no ornate giant compass. Twelve main surface labels and two underground labels, all spelled accurately, placed on terrain clearings or subtle pale knockouts, never large dark label plaques. Fine labels should be part of an exceptionally readable map. One tiny quiet key for rest, dungeon and lift symbols if room permits. No lore paragraphs, no giant title, no UI dashboard, no numbering every tree, no cartoon character tokens. Only meaningful cartographic content.

AVOID THE REJECTED LOOK: no photoreal 3D diorama, no giant luminous tree dominating the continent, no giant statue at the center, no endless Gothic arches, no dozens of impossible aqueducts, no gold glow from every cave, no random waterfalls, no uniform red trees, no perfectly round boss arenas everywhere, no generic symmetric biome theme park. Prioritize geographical cause-and-effect, working settlements, visual restraint, uneven density and spaces worth exploring. No real game's names on the map. No watermark.
