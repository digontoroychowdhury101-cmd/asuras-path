# Bellwater — revised world concept

Working setting name, not a proposed final game title. This is a world design for review, not an implemented Roblox map. It replaces the earlier seven-landmark concept. The sanctuary remains the social lobby, and its adjoining duel court retains optional one-versus-one play.

## Direction

Fourteen regions contain forty-two named exploration areas. Twelve regions are above ground; two form a connected underground network. Size comes from distinct places, routes, encounters and discoveries, rather than multiplying scenery.

The landscape should look occupied before it looks spectacular. Fields have boundaries. Villages have water, work and roads. A quarry supplies the city's pale masonry. Wealthy streets stand above flooded workers' districts. Roof tiles, drains and foundation repairs reveal different periods of construction. Large landmarks are occasional and memorable.

The new map image is an ink-and-wash atlas: readable terrain, restrained pigments, imperfect coastlines, ordinary buildings, and clear paths. It is not another miniature kingdom made entirely of cathedral ruins. Regional names and broad relationships in the written plan take precedence over incidental details in the generated illustration.

## Reference principles

These are design interpretations of the references, rather than claims that this layout reproduces their systems.

- **Elden Ring:** alternate open exploration with substantial, multi-level dungeon spaces; give the player several promising destinations and the freedom to leave a difficult encounter. Bandai Namco describes the connection between its overworld and complex legacy dungeons in its [official overview](https://en.bandainamcoent.eu/elden-ring/news/elden-ring-enter-the-lands-between-101-trailer).
- **Sekiro:** design height as usable space: routes through roofs, cliff ledges, galleries and ravines. Its [official description](https://www.sekirothegame.com/uk/en/) identifies vertical traversal as a central feature. This proposal does not assume a grappling hook; all essential routes must work with the movement system we actually build.
- **Bloodborne:** give city districts a specific social history, unsettling street detail and tightly controlled atmosphere. Japan Studio's [official introduction](https://blog.playstation.com/2014/06/10/bloodborne-coming-exclusively-to-ps4-in-2015-new-details/) connects its city, pilgrimage, architecture and environmental detail.

The cultures and geography here are original. Mountain districts and lowland districts belong to the same kingdom; they should share construction traditions and trade goods instead of looking like unrelated games joined together.

## The history written into the landscape

The crown drained an inland lake to uncover an older city beneath its bed. The engineering project flooded the downstream wards while opening new land to royal claim. The court prospered on the high ridge; people below lived with the damage.

The excavators eventually broke through structures that had been sealed before the kingdom existed. Now water is rising from beneath the exposed city, even when the mountain rivers run dry.

Early exploration shows flood marks, stranded boats, abandoned work camps and official notices blaming a storm. The middle game exposes the engineering decisions and competing accounts. The final descent reveals what the original seals contained. Item descriptions and NPC testimony disagree; the physical geography remains consistent.

## Geographic rules

- The sea is west. Wreckers' Coast follows its cliffs and the estuary.
- Snowy headwaters rise in the north and northeast. Rivers descend through Pine March and Thousand Steps.
- The former inland lake occupies the east. Its surviving waters and exposed bed feed Reed Country to the south.
- Great Sluice controls the western outlet of the basin. Its drainage channel runs through the Old Ward and Bellwater Town toward the coast.
- High Ward sits on the ridge above the outlet, explaining both its security and its view over the lower city.
- Longfields occupies the gentler southern upland. Quarry Hills cuts into the western foothills.
- Deepworks lies below the dam and capital. First City lies below the eastern lakebed. They connect underground; they are not separate magical destinations.
- The sanctuary is one defensible refuge within Bellwater Town. The whole world does not radiate out of it through equal bridges.

## Progression and discoveries

**Opening:** Longfields establishes combat and readable landmarks. Bellwater Town introduces the sanctuary and optional duels. The coast, wetlands and lower city offer choices before the first major dungeon commitment.

**Middle:** the player can advance through the Old Ward into the High Ward, or take the quarry and forest route into the upper city. Weirlands and the Salt Basin offer different routes into the central mystery. Clearing every region is not mandatory.

**Late:** mountain and underground discoveries supply different pieces of the seal's history. Reaching the final court requires knowledge earned through these routes, rather than three identical region tokens. The exact quest conditions need separate gameplay design.

**Returns:** the convict stair, ore lift, city cistern and sanctuary well should each produce a recognizable return to a place the player remembers. Show the closed side well before it can be opened. A shortcut's landing should have a distinctive sound, object or view.

**Optional content:** Beacon Fort, Shuttered Baths, the quarry pursuit and the forest fort carry side stories and equipment. They must have different spatial ideas. Do not repeat the same three-room catacomb under different names.

**PvP:** the sanctuary's duel court has its own clear entrance and queue. Story paths never require entering it. Duel geometry must support both players with fair starts and clear boundaries.

## How the world should feel from the ground

1. From Shepherds' Rise, see the capital's copper roofs and the dam as two separate destinations. A roadside village provides ordinary scale.
2. From Old Ward, see sunlight on High Ward while flood stains and blocked doors sit at eye level. The route upward has an understandable physical purpose.
3. In Thousand Steps, look back through timber galleries and recognize the dam far below. Wind, joinery and steep terrain provide identity without changing to an unrelated culture.
4. Enter the Salt Basin along what was once a pier. The pier now ends high over dry ground, revealing the former waterline without explanation text.
5. Near the end, emerge from a wet service stair beside the sanctuary well. The hub's familiar bell can be heard before the player sees daylight.

## Art constraints

- Use limestone, dark local brick, weathered cedar and copper consistently, with regional changes in use and wealth.
- Keep most vegetation naturally green, brown or seasonal ochre. Reserve the sanctuary's red foliage for that particular landmark.
- Give each district a few specific working objects: tanning racks, lime kilns, survey equipment, pump chains, stranded mooring posts. Place them for a reason.
- Preserve quieter fields, untidy backs of buildings, bare rock and ordinary streets between major sights.
- Avoid repeated cathedral silhouettes, symmetrical biome wedges, endless decorative bridges, uniformly wet surfaces, universal orange glow and waterfalls with no watershed.
- Do not use a giant luminous tree or floating statue as the entire world's organizing symbol. The sanctuary can retain its own distinctive tree and statue.
- Boss arenas should be believable places before the encounter begins: a granary, a yard, a survey hall, a pump chamber or a defensive wall. Vary shape, light, sound and approach.

## Roblox build approach

This document is the full-world direction. Build a complete playable section first: Longfields, Bellwater Town and the first Old Ward route, including the sanctuary, one meaningful boss encounter and one returning shortcut. Validate movement, combat readability and travel rhythm on touch, controller and mouse before setting the remaining world's physical scale.

The atlas is not a measured construction plan. Essential next design work is a route blockout with actual jump distances, doorway widths, sightlines, checkpoints, enemy positions, loading boundaries and camera tests. Region scale and travel times should come from that blockout. Avoid shrinking the whole atlas into a compact decorative park.

Use ordinary geometry and terrain for the route blockout. Reserve detailed meshes for architectural pieces that materially improve silhouette or surface quality. The current project has not been converted or modified by this concept work.
